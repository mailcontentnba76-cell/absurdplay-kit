[energetic, fast-paced narrator] What if every NBA team was its actual mascot?

Small problem. The Lakers don't have a mascot. Neither do the Knicks. Two of the biggest teams in the league... nothing. So we gave them one.

The Lakers started in Minnesota. The Land of ten thousand lakes. In 1960 they moved to L.A. and kept the name. L.A. County has twelve lakes. Twelve. But lakes have ducks. Meet the Lakers.

The Knicks? In 1946, they pulled their name out of a hat. Knickerbockers. After Father Knickerbocker. An old Dutch New Yorker in rolled-up pants. He was their very first logo.

On paper? The Lakers have seventeen titles. The Knicks are the defending champions. One of these teams is a duck.

Tip-off. Ducks can fly. Old men can't. Dunk. Dunk. Dunk. Lakers up ten.

Knicks timeout. Father Knickerbocker draws up a play. It's from 1809. It's mostly walking.

Then he remembers. He brought bread. Every duck stops. Knicks go on a run. Twelve straight.

Halftime. It's November. Two ducks fly south for the winter. The Lakers are down to three.

Second half. The ducks get the ball back. No hands. They just waddle with it. Travel. Travel. Travel. Knicks lead by one.

Last shot. The old men are tired. Their pants are from the sixteen hundreds. One duck takes off... and drops the ball from above.

Seventeen titles, or the guy in the pants? Comment who wins.
